1. State/path functions and the First Law

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
ΔE = Ef − EiOnly changes in E are used, never absolute E. State functions: P, V, T, E, H, S, G, A. Path functions: q, w (so write q, not Δq)."Only q and w are Path; everything else is State."
ΔE = q + wMaster equation. Heat in +, heat out −. Work done ON system +, work done BY system −.Bank account: money coming in is +.
Isolated: ΔE = 0
Cyclic: ΔE = 0, q = −w
Const V: ΔE = qv
Adiabatic: ΔE = w		Pick the special case from the keyword in the question.Keyword → term that vanishes: isolated (q, w), cyclic (ΔE), isochoric (w), adiabatic (q).
w = −PΔV = −ΔngRTExpansion work for gas reactions in an open beaker. Closed rigid vessel: w = 0.Expansion = system loses energy, so the minus sign.
Deck examples: motor 15 kJ/s with 2 kJ/s lost → ΔE = −17 kJ; spring +100 J, −15 J → +85 J. Fe + HCl, 63 g, open beaker → w ≈ −2.79 kJ (closed: 0).

2. Enthalpy

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
H = E + PV
ΔH = ΔE + PΔV		Definition. H is extensive and a state function."H = E + PV" (Eat PiVa).
ΔH = qpHeat measured at constant pressure (open flask, all lab reactions).p-lab → qp = ΔH. Bomb calorimeter → qv = ΔE.
ΔH = ΔE + ΔngRT
Δng = ngas products − ngas reactants		Convert between ΔH and ΔE. Count only gases; ignore (l) and (s). Use R = 8.314 J/mol·K, T in K, convert kJ to J."Products minus Reactants, Gases only". If Δng = 0 then ΔH = ΔE (the Dec-2024 end-sem PYQ).
Phase change at 1 atm: w = RT, ΔE = ΔH − RTVaporisation: liquid volume is negligible, so PΔV = RT.Evaporation: ΔE is smaller than ΔH by RT.
Deck examples: CH₄ formation, ΔH = −74.85 kJ → ΔE = −72.37 kJ (Δng = −1). Heptane, qv = −4793 kJ, Δng = −4 → ΔH = −4802.9 kJ. Br₂ vaporisation: ΔH = 29.2, ΔE = 26.44 kJ/mol.

3. Heat capacity

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
C = q/ΔT = q/(T₂ − T₁)Heat needed per degree rise."Heat per degree."
Cp − Cv = nR
Cp,m − Cv,m = R		Switch between Cp and Cv for an ideal gas. R = 8.314 J or 1.987 cal.Cp is bigger (extra work to expand), so "p minus v = R".

4. Entropy: definition, standard entropy, 3rd law

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
ΔS = Sf − SiIsolated system: ΔS > 0 means spontaneous.Disorder wins: isolated + spontaneous → ΔS up.
ΔS = qrev/TReversible, isothermal heat flow. Phase changes: ΔS = ΔHtrans/T at the transition temperature (K)."q over T": heat in is +, heat out is −. Always use K.
ΔS° = ΣS°(products) − ΣS°(reactants)Entropy change of a reaction from tables. Multiply each S° by its stoichiometric coefficient.Always "Products − Reactants".
S°f = S°(compound) − ΣS°(elements)Entropy of formation.Same pattern, elements on the reactant side.
3rd law: S = 0 for a perfect crystal at 0 KS° > 0 for any substance above 0 K. Units J mol⁻¹K⁻¹; 1 eu = 4.184 J/mol·K."Perfect crystal, zero kelvin, zero entropy."
Deck examples: water, 9650 cal/mol at 373 K → 25.87 cal/K·mol. Ice melting, 80×18 cal at 273 K → 5.274 cal/K·mol. Urea hydrolysis ΔS° = 84.81 cal/K. S°f(CO₂) = 2.86 J/K.

5. Entropy change of an ideal gas

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
ΔS = nCv ln(T₂/T₁) + nR ln(V₂/V₁)Given T and V."Volume goes with Cv."
ΔS = nCp ln(T₂/T₁) + nR ln(P₁/P₂)Given T and P. Note the pressure ratio is inverted."Pressure goes with Cp", and P is flipped because V ∝ 1/P.
Isothermal: ΔS = nR ln(V₂/V₁) = nR ln(P₁/P₂)
Isobaric: ΔS = nCp ln(T₂/T₁)
Isochoric: ΔS = nCv ln(T₂/T₁)		Delete the term that is constant. If the sheet gives log₁₀, multiply by 2.303.Constant T kills the T-term; constant P or V picks Cp or Cv.
Deck examples: 5 mol, V×6 at 330 K → 74.5 J/K. 1 mol heated 20→40 °C at const P, Cp = 6.189 → 0.4087 cal/K. 1 mol, 15 L/2 atm → 50 L/1 atm → 5.156 cal/K.
Check: expansion gives ΔS > 0 (V₂ > V₁ or P₁ > P₂); compression gives ΔS < 0.

6. Entropy of mixing

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
ΔmixS = −R Σ ni ln χi = −nR Σ χi ln χiMixing ideal gases at constant T, P. χi = ni/ntotal. Add one term per gas, however many gases there are.The minus sign cancels the negative ln χ, so the answer is always positive.
Max at χ₁ = χ₂ = 0.5Proof question (PYQ): put x₁ = x, x₂ = 1 − x, set d(ΔS)/dx = 0, which gives ln[x/(1 − x)] = 0, so x = 0.5."Equal parts mix the messiest."
Mid-sem Autumn 2025: 1 CO₂ + 1 N₂ + 2 O₂ at 300 K → −R(1 ln¼ + 1 ln¼ + 2 ln½) = 34.6 J/K.

7. Free energy (G) and work function (A)

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
A = E − TS; ΔA = ΔE − TΔS
G = H − TS; ΔG = ΔH − TΔS		ΔG decides spontaneity at constant T, P. A is the same idea at constant T, V."G goes with H, A goes with E" (G-H, A-E).
−ΔA = wmax (reversible work done by system)Maximum work obtainable (Helmholtz energy).A = "Achievable work".
−ΔG = w − PΔV = net (non-expansion) workUseful work, e.g. electrical work from a cell.Free energy = what is left after paying the expansion tax.
ΔG < 0 spontaneous; = 0 equilibrium; > 0 non-spontaneousFour cases: (ΔH−, ΔS+) always spontaneous. (ΔH+, ΔS−) never. (−, −) spontaneous at low T. (+, +) spontaneous at high T."Low T: ΔH decides. High T: TΔS decides." Same signs means temperature decides.

8. G with pressure and temperature

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
dG = V dP − S dTParent equation. Const T: dG = V dP. Const P: dG = −S dT."VP minus ST" (V then P, S then T).
(∂G/∂P)T = V; (∂G/∂T)P = −SRead off from dG. Entropy is the negative slope of G vs T.The variable you differentiate by is the partner of the one you keep.
ΔG = nRT ln(P₂/P₁) = 2.303 nRT log(P₂/P₁)Isothermal change of pressure for an ideal gas, so it works for reversible or irreversible paths (G is a state function).P₂ over P₁: compression gives ΔG > 0.
ΔG° = ΣΔG°f(products) − ΣΔG°f(reactants)Standard reaction free energy. ΔG°f of an element is 0.Products − Reactants, again.
ΔS = (ΔH − ΔG)/TRearranged ΔG = ΔH − TΔS to find ΔS; then reuse it for ΔG at another T.Solve for ΔS first, then plug in the new T.
Deck examples: 4 mol, 10→1 atm, 298 K → −22823 J. ΔG = −16, ΔH = −10 kcal at 300 K → ΔS = 20 cal/K, ΔG(330 K) = −16.6 kcal. Water → steam at 100 °C, 1→0.5 atm → −513.82 cal (use R = 1.987; the phase change itself has ΔG = 0). CO + ½O₂ → CO₂: ΔG° = −257.2 kJ.

9. Gibbs-Helmholtz equation

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
ΔG = ΔH + T[∂(ΔG)/∂T]PMain form. Given ΔG at two temperatures, slope ≈ (ΔG₂ − ΔG₁)/(T₂ − T₁) and ΔG at the middle T ≈ average. Then solve for ΔH."G = H + T·slope". Check units: the slope in J/K must become kJ/K.
[∂(G/T)/∂T]P = −H/T²
[∂(ΔG/T)/∂T]P = −ΔH/T²		Derivation and "show that" questions. Start from G = H − TS, divide by T², use the quotient rule."H over T-squared with a minus."
ΔA = ΔE + T[∂(ΔA)/∂T]VSame equation with A, E, and constant V.Swap G→A, H→E, P→V.
Deck examples: ΔH = −264.93 kJ, slope 19.58 J/K at 373 K → ΔG = −257.63 kJ. ΔG = −91.21 (293 K), −89.12 (303 K) → slope 0.209 kJ/K, ΔG(298) = −90.165 → ΔH = −152.447 kJ. Practice slide (−86 at 298 K, −84 at 308 K, find ΔH at 303 K): slope = 0.2 kJ/K, avg ΔG = −85, ΔH = −85 − 303(0.2) = −145.6 kJ (my working, not shown in the deck).

Application to cells (Daniel cell)

ΔG = −nFEElectrical work = nFE. Use for E.M.F. ↔ ΔG."G equals minus nFE" (n-F-E).
ΔS = nF(∂E/∂T)PEntropy from temperature coefficient of E.M.F.ΔS = −∂ΔG/∂T gives the sign flip.
ΔH = −nF[E − T(∂E/∂T)P]Enthalpy of cell reaction.Substitute ΔG = −nFE into Gibbs-Helmholtz.

10. Van't Hoff isotherm

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
G = G° + RT ln PFree energy per mole of a gas at pressure P.Standard value plus "RT ln P".
ΔG = ΔG° + RT ln [(PCcPDd)/(PAaPBb)]ΔG at any non-equilibrium pressures. The log term is the reaction quotient Qp.Products over reactants, each raised to its coefficient.
ΔG° = −RT ln K = −2.303 RT log KAt equilibrium ΔG = 0. Use to get K from ΔG° (or the reverse).ΔG° negative → K > 1; ΔG° positive → K < 1.
Deck examples: ΔG° = 71.128 kJ at 2000 K → log Kp = −1.857, Kp = 0.0139. ΔG° = 1×10⁵ J at 673 K → Kp = 1.737×10⁻⁸.

11. Van't Hoff isochore (K vs T)

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
ln K = −ΔH°/RT + ΔS°/RStraight line: ln K vs 1/T has slope −ΔH°/R and intercept ΔS°/R."y = mx + c" with x = 1/T.
ln(K₂/K₁) = (ΔH°/R)(T₂ − T₁)/(T₁T₂)
log(K₂/K₁) = ΔH°/(2.303R) · (T₂ − T₁)/(T₁T₂)		Find K₂ at a new T, or find ΔH° from two K values. Then ΔG° = −2.303RT log K and ΔS° = (ΔH° − ΔG°)/T.Subscript 2 on top everywhere. Endothermic (ΔH > 0): K rises with T.
Deck examples: Kp = 10⁻¹² at 600 K, 10⁻⁷ at 700 K → ΔH = 402.09 kJ. H₂ + S ⇌ H₂S, K = 18.5 (925 K), 9.25 (1000 K) → ΔH° = −71.08 kJ, ΔG° = −22.4 kJ, ΔS° = −52.6 J/K.

12. Clapeyron and Clausius-Clapeyron

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
dP/dT = ΔH/[T(VB − VA)] = ΔS/ΔVAny two-phase equilibrium of one substance (melting, vaporisation). Derived from GA = GB and dG = VdP − SdT, with ΔS = ΔH/T."Slope of P-T line = ΔH over TΔV".
d ln P/dT = ΔHv/RT²Liquid ⇌ vapour: Vv ≫ Vl and Vv = RT/P (ideal).Looks like the isochore: ΔH/RT².
ln(P₂/P₁) = (ΔHv/R)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂) = (ΔHv/R)(T₂ − T₁)/(T₁T₂)
log₁₀(P₂/P₁) = ΔHv/(2.303R) · (…)		Three uses: (1) find ΔHv; (2) find boiling point at a new pressure; (3) find vapour pressure at a new T. With R = 1.987 cal, 2.303R = 4.576.Higher T goes with higher P. If P₂ > P₁ then T₂ > T₁, so check your answer's direction.
Normal boiling point means P = 760 mmHg (given implicitly). Use K, keep P units the same on both sides, and match R to the energy unit (8.314 J / 0.008314 kJ / 1.987 cal).
Deck answers: water, 634 mm (95 °C) and 760 mm (100 °C) → ΔHv = 9886 cal/mol. Water boils at 374 K (101 °C) under 787 mm. Water at 90 °C → 52.88 cm. 1-propanol at 52.8 °C → 100.2 torr. Compound, 24.3 torr (273 K), 135 torr (325 K) → 24.3 kJ/mol. Liquid with 6.91 mmHg at 0 °C, n.b.p. 105 °C → 38.4 kJ/mol. CCl₄ → 31.4 kJ/mol. Hexane at 25 °C → 171 torr.

13. Partial molar properties and chemical potential

FormulaWhere / how to useTrick
X̄i = (∂X/∂ni)T,P,njContribution of 1 mole of component i to extensive property X (V, E, H, S, G, A) in a mixture.Hold T, P and the other moles fixed.
X = n₁X̄₁ + n₂X̄₂ + …Total property of a mixture from partial molar values."Total = Σ moles × partial".
μi = (∂G/∂ni)T,P,nj = ḠiChemical potential: intensive, drives matter from high μ to low μ, equal everywhere at equilibrium.μ = "escaping tendency".
dG = −S dT + V dP + Σ μi dni
(G)T,P = Σ niμi		Open systems (composition changes).dG = VdP − SdT plus a "μ dn" term.
Σ ni dμi = 0Gibbs-Duhem at constant T, P. If one μ rises, another must fall."Sum of n dμ is zero".
(∂μi/∂T)P = −S̄i
(∂μi/∂P)T = V̄i		Variation of μ with T and P (from mixed second derivatives of G).Same pattern as (∂G/∂T) = −S and (∂G/∂P) = V.
μ = μ° + RT ln P
Δμ = RT ln(P₂/P₁)		Ideal gas change of μ with pressure.Same as ΔG for 1 mole.
